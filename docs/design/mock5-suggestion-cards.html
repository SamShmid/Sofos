What's new, in plain words

  1. 1Person-first cardsAvatar + name top-left, time next to it, kind label ("change" / "new task" / "removal") top-right in navy. The sentence "Dana proposes…" becomes the card's structure instead of prose.
  2. 2The target on its own lineWhat kind of thing and its name, bolded — no more hunting through a sentence.
  3. 3Only what changes, colon formatYour ruling: colons not arrows. Unchanged fields never shown; internal names never shown.
  4. 4Approval-time name checkYour new ruling both ways: a duplicate name is rejected when the suggester proposes it, AND re-checked at approval — if it collides by then, Approve shows this error and the card stays pending.
  5. 5Suggesters lose the fake review queueYour ruling: they see their own list with statuses (pending / approved / rejected + Retract), never "Needs your review".
  6. 7Multi-place edits → View changesSamuel's ruling: when one suggestion touches SEVERAL places in a note, the card doesn't offer all-or-nothing Approve/Reject — it says "edits in 3 places" with a View changes button that opens the note itself in review mode.
  7. 8Single-place edits stay on the cardOne contiguous change still shows its little diff right on the card with plain Approve/Reject — no detour for the simple case.
  8. 9Review mode: highlights in the text, cards in the marginSamuel's ruling: edits stay highlighted in place (struck-through old, highlighted new), but the approve/reject controls move OFF the text into a reserved right-hand column, Google-Docs style. Each margin card aligns with its highlight; clicking either focuses the other. Resolved edits show a quiet "applied" tag inline.
  9. 10Per-edit cards in the marginOne card per edit — proposer, time, the old → new text, Approve / Reject. Each edit is its own decision ("technically different suggestions, even in the same file" — Samuel). The top bar still offers Approve-remaining / Reject-remaining.
  10. 11Comments — new conceptWriters AND suggesters can attach a comment to any stretch of text. Comments never change the file; the commented text gets a soft gold highlight and the comment sits in the margin with Reply and Resolve. (Gold is a placeholder — needs Samuel's color ruling, since the palette reserves azure and bans orange.)
  11. 6Editing / Suggesting switchYour ruling from the P48 round: writers can flip to Suggesting; their edits become cards; they may approve their own. Lives in the workspace/project header.
